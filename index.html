Hello

I'm Sung Hyun "Sung". I am a PhD candidate in The Media School at Indiana University, with a minor in Human-Computer Interaction/Design.

I study how people judge and form relationships with communicators that offer only limited cues, and how those judgments shape trust, self-disclosure, and consumer behavior. My dissertation asks why people disclose personal information to AI systems faster and more deeply than to other people, and what that means for us. As a mixed-method scholar, I work with lab experiments, surveys, computational text analysis, and in-depth interviews.

Before graduate school, I spent thirteen years in Korean media and entertainment. At CJ Corporation and CJ ENM, I led brand strategy and consumer experience design across theme park development, media commerce, and global campaigns including KCON. All of my work was about how audiences encounter unfamiliar(a.k.a something new) content and decide whether to share their time and passion in it—my academic journey is also on the line with this.

Twitch vs YouTube: Exploring how synchronicity is associated with social interaction and positive emotion among gaming video viewers
Chae, S. W., Alberta, M & Lee, S. H.
Entertainment Computing, 2025
pdf
This study explores how synchronicity is associated with users' social interaction and positive emotion when watching gaming content on video-based social media platforms. YouTube and Twitch were selected as comparable venues wherein asynchronous and synchronous communication can be observed, respectively. To control the effect of video content, we found a gaming video on YouTube, the content of which had been originally streamed on Twitch. From the identical videos on the two platforms, the asynchronous comments on YouTube and the synchronous chat messages on Twitch were collected. We analyzed the two data sets using the text analysis program LIWC. The results showed that users are more likely to use social words in the asynchronous setting than in the synchronous setting. Meanwhile, positive emotion was more frequently observed in Twitch's synchronous texts compared to YouTube's asynchronous texts. These findings suggest the possibility that Twitch users are more like eSports spectators than chatters.
Beyond Prompt Engineering: Exploring Collaborative Dialogue with GenAI for Problem-Solving
Lee, S. H., Velez, J. A., & Noh, D.
Cyberpsychology, Behavior, and Social Networking, 2025
pdf website
This study investigated how different discourse practices (construction, co-construction, and constructive conflict) in human-AI interaction influence collaborative problem-solving with ChatGPT on creative writing tasks. Discourse practices (how humans talk to the AI) significantly predict shared cognition (finding common ground). Shared cognition mediates the link between discourse practices and perceived team effectiveness. Shared cognition positively influences the intention to continue using ChatGPT (in line with the Technology Acceptance Model). The study suggest that human-to-human communication principles are vital for effective human-AI collaboration. The study proposes that ChatGPT may be capable of mirroring these positive discourse practices, opening the door to optimizing dialogue beyond simple prompt engineering.
Trolling is in the eye of the beholder: Perceptions of trolling motivations in others predict vigilantism in video games
Velez, J. A., Yoo, H., Smith, X., Lee, S. H., Lin, Y., & Chae, S. W.
Communication Research, 2025
pdf website
Trolling in online video games, while done by a few, is a growing problem. This study investigated how players perceive trolling and why they retaliate. Players determined if a behavior was trolling by weighing auxiliary cues (like game rules, the target, and community norms) associated with the behavior to attribute malicious motivations. The perception of trolling directly increased a player's desire to retaliate with harassment. The study examine that to better understand the impact of trolling, researchers should focus on players' reactions and their interpretations of others' motivations, rather than just the behavior itself.
Where do cross-cutting discussions happen?: Identifying cross-cutting comments on YouTube videos of political vloggers and mainstream news outlets
Chae, S. W., & Lee, S. H.
PLOS ONE, 2024
pdf website
This study investigated the extent of political echo chambers versus cross-cutting discussions on YouTube video comments, comparing political vloggers to mainstream news outlets using both manual analysis (for vlogger comments) and NLP classifiers (for mainstream news comments) to determine political leanings. The finding is the proportion of cross-cutting discussions (comments from opposing political views) significantly varied based on both the channel's political leaning and the media type (vlogger vs. mainstream news). That is, results suggest neutral news outlets may serve as a potential venue for cross-cutting discussions.
Sharing emotion while spectating video game play: Twitch users' emotional change, COVID-19 pandemic
Chae, S. W. & Lee, S. H.
Computers in Human Behavior, 2022
pdf website
Investigates emotional sharing and community building on Twitch during the COVID-19 pandemic, revealing how gaming platforms served as emotional support systems and fostered social connection during isolation.
We're All in This Together: Legitimacy and Coronavirus-Oriented CSR Messaging
Browning, N., Lee, E., Lee, S. H., & Yang, S. U.
Sustainability, 2022
pdf website
This study investigates how legitimization strategies embedded in CSR messages related to the COVID-19 pandemic influenced multidimensional stakeholder assessments of reputation. The results of this 3 × 2 × 2 experimental survey, which manipulated pragmatic and moral legitimacy using three conditions (self- vs. other- vs. both-oriented messaging); substantive and symbolic management (informative vs. uninformative content); and popularization and standardization approaches (leadership vs. followership), indicate that popularization strategies communicated substantively and standardization strategies communicated symbolically generally yield the greatest reputational gains. More nuanced findings from three-way interaction effects are further discussed, with an emphasis on the role of double-sided messages seeking to simultaneously establish pragmatic and moral legitimacy.
